# How the grade is calculated

Read this only when the user asks why they got their grade. Explain it in plain words; don't paste the table unless they want the detail.

The score (0–100) is a weighted average of four parts. Parts that can't be measured yet are skipped and the remaining weights are scaled up.

| Part | Weight | What it measures | Full marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency cushion | 30% | Liquid savings ÷ monthly spending | 6+ months |
| Debt health | 30% | Monthly debt payments ÷ monthly income (or debt ÷ assets if no income); −20 if any debt is at 10%+ annual interest | No debt, or payments under ~10% of income with nothing high-interest |
| Savings rate | 25% | (Income − spending) ÷ income | 30%+ |
| Momentum | 15% | Underlying net worth change per month ÷ monthly spending (needs 2+ check-ins) | Growing by half a month's spending or more per month |

**Grade:** A ≥ 85 · B ≥ 70 · C ≥ 55 · D ≥ 40 · F < 40

## Net worth

- Net worth = assets (your ownership share of each) + money owed to you − all debts.
- Money owed to you counts in full if "likely", half if "unsure", zero if "unlikely".
- Assets fall into classes: cash & deposits, investments, retirement & insurance, real estate, vehicles, business, personal valuables. Only cash & deposits marked liquid count toward the emergency cushion.

## Revaluations and Momentum

Real estate, vehicles, business stakes and valuables are valued by estimate, not by statement. Holdings in another currency also move when the exchange rate moves, even if nothing about them changed. When the estimate of something held at both check-ins changes — a new appraisal of the land, a car losing value — that change is a **revaluation**. Momentum uses the **underlying change**: the change in net worth minus revaluations. So:

- A higher land estimate raises net worth but not the grade.
- A weaker home currency raises the value of US ETFs, but that FX gain is excluded too; their price changes still count.
- Paying down a mortgage or car loan from savings is neutral (cash down, debt down).
- Buying or selling property is treated as a swap, not a revaluation; only any gain or loss on the sale shows up.

## Observations that don't change the score

Flags also note a single asset above 60% of total assets, mostly-illiquid wealth, loans larger than the asset securing them, and property values with no valuation date or older than 18 months.

## Notes

- Monthly spending is the average of recent check-ins (normalized to 30 days), or the onboarding estimate.
- Age, risk tolerance and personality do not change the grade. They shape the advice, because a 22-year-old and a 55-year-old with the same numbers need different next steps.
- The benchmarks are common rules of thumb, not universal truths. Someone with a very stable job may reasonably hold less than six months; a freelancer may want more.
